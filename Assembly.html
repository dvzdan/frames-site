Assembly

Assembly Instructions

Before following the steps, it helps to understand the little chain reaction inside the frame. The instructions will walk you through the setup, but the mechanism is forgiving. Once you understand what each part is trying to do, you can use the steps as both a guide and a checklist.

Tools and preparationTweezers for everyone; preparation tools only when you print parts or prepare images yourself.

Tools to have ready

What you need depends on which parts you're preparing yourself.

For everyone assembling

  • Tweezers - helpful for grabbing and positioning the string and image paper in tight spots.

If you printed the parts yourself

  • Pliers - for removing the upper built-in supports.
  • Small flush cutters - a short, angled pair is strongly preferred for removing the lower supports.
  • Gel super glue - for attaching the lid to the weight carriage.

Ready-to-Assemble parts arrive cleaned, and the weight-carriage lid is already glued.

If you're preparing your own images

  • Single-hole punch - handy for making the paper slot in the Cover Image.

If you choose printed-and-cut images, you do not need a hole punch.

Understand the mechanismSee how the clock, string, zipper, latch, weight, and cover image create the reveal.

How the mechanism works:

  • Clock mechanism sits inside the frame.
  • Capstan presses onto the clock mechanism.
  • String ties to the capstan.
    • Then runs through the clock/string guide hole.
    • And through the top-left eyelet.
    • Then ties to the zipper anchor.
  • Zipper head supports the latch.
  • Latch supports the weight.
  • Weight is tethered to the sliding cover image.
  • Cover image sits over the reveal image.
  • When the timer runs, the clock winds the string, pulls the zipper, releases the latch, drops the weight, slides the cover image up, and reveals the image underneath.

Easy-to-miss checks:

Assembly guide is loading.

Troubleshooting and FAQ

How do I reset the mechanism?

Return to the assembly instructions and repeat the steps that set the mechanism in motion. You will need to redo some of the assembly process, but not all of it.

Threading the string is a nuisance.

It can be. Stiffen the end with a small drop of superglue, or glue the supplied tiny metal wire to the end of the string and use it as a threading needle.

I'm worried I assembled it incorrectly and it won't work.

The design and assembly instructions include multiple layers of redundancy. Most individual steps are not essential on their own; instead, they work together to reduce the overall likelihood of failure to below 1%. If you get one detail slightly wrong, the mechanism will usually still work - you have most likely increased the risk of failure only marginally.
